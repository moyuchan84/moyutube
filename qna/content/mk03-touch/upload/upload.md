A never-dated geek uncle gets caught holding his own hand at night. "It's an experiment. Result: nothing." So why does only someone else's hand feel special? He explains, conference-talk style.

- You can't tickle yourself: your brain predicts your own touch and turns it down
- Someone else's touch lights up the brain's emotional areas; your own quiets them (the difference shows up in the spinal cord)
- Your skin has slow nerves (C-tactile fibers) that respond best to gentle stroking
